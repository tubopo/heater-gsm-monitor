A text message when the heating stops.

Heater GSM Monitor is a small open-source device for places you can't check on every day: a summer house, a workshop, a pump room. It watches one temperature and texts you when it leaves the safe range. No WiFi, no app, no subscription. Just a SIM card.

How it works

Three things, and nothing else.

1 · MEASURE

Reads the temperature every five minutes

A single bad reading is ignored. Three in a row are reported as a disconnected sensor, and a probe you plug back in is picked up on its own.

2 · ALERT

Texts you when something changes

When the temperature leaves the normal range you get one SMS with the reading and the status. Another follows only if the situation changes.

3 · ASK

Answers a call with a text

Call its number whenever you want to know. It hangs up without answering and replies with the current temperature by SMS.

StatusDefault range
NORMAL10 – 45 °C
WARNING5 – 10 °C or 45 – 55 °C
CRITICAL5 °C and below, 55 °C and above
DISCONNECTEDProbe missing or unreadable

Entering WARNING, CRITICAL or DISCONNECTED sends one SMS; returning to NORMAL sends none. All four thresholds are settings.

Built to be left alone

Most of the work went into what happens when things go wrong at 3 a.m. and nobody is there.

A fixed SMS budget

At most three alerts an hour and ten a day, at least three minutes apart. A flapping sensor can't drain the SIM, and the daily count survives a power cut.

Late, not lost

An alert that can't go out yet, because there is no signal or the budget is used up, waits and is sent when it can be, with the reading at that moment.

No WiFi required

The everyday firmware has no WiFi, no cloud account and no remote update. It needs mobile coverage and power, nothing else.

Thresholds with a margin

A warning that starts below 10 °C clears at 11 °C. A temperature hovering at a threshold gives you one message instead of ten.

A screen that stays dark

The display is off until you touch the button, then shows temperature, signal and status for thirty seconds.

A service mode when you need it

A separate debug firmware opens its own hotspot with a status page, live logs and a test-SMS button, for the day you are standing next to it.

What it is made of

Common hobby parts and a few YAML files. The firmware is built with ESPHome.

  • ESP8266 NodeMCU — the controller
  • SIM800L — the GSM modem that sends and receives
  • DS18B20 — the temperature probe
  • SSD1306 OLED, 128×64 — the status screen
  • TTP223 — the touch button that wakes the screen
  • LM2596 step-down and a 2200 µF capacitor — steady power for the modem
  • A SIM card — with SMS, and its PIN switched off

Worth knowing before you build one

  • It is a DIY project. You solder it, flash it over USB and edit a configuration file.
  • The SIM800L is a 2G modem. Check that 2G still works where the device will live.
  • Messages cost what your operator charges. The SMS budget is there partly for that reason.
  • It is not a certified alarm. Treat it as an extra pair of eyes, not the only protection for something that matters.
